# Executive Summary

## Professional thesis

Joshua Upshaw is a clinical data scientist and cognitive neuroscientist whose work centers on a common technical problem: **how to convert complex human data into evidence that is reliable enough to support a decision**.

His research progression spans behavioral and affective science, controlled human experiments, EEG/ERP, spectral neural dynamics, 1000 Hz eye tracking, neuroimaging, and NSF-funded clinical AI using longitudinal oncology EHR data.

## What differentiates the work

**1. Measurement before modeling.**
The first question is whether the variable or label is valid, available at the relevant time, and measured consistently.

**2. Multimodal reasoning.**
Behavior, neural signals, gaze, pupil dynamics, neuroimaging, clinical text, structured data, and workflow events are treated as complementary evidence rather than interchangeable measurements.

**3. Validation under realistic conditions.**
Random retrospective performance is not treated as sufficient when the real decision occurs prospectively or over time.

**4. Null and weak findings change the next experiment.**
The portfolio includes unsupported hypotheses, small effects, and observational limitations because they often provide the most useful design information.

**5. Research is translated into action.**
At Hidalga, retrospective findings were converted into explicit requirements for telemetry, silent evaluation, human review, and prospective pilot endpoints.

**6. Evidence is communicated for the audience without changing its meaning.**
The broader record includes first-author publications, peer review, university teaching, IRB service, scientific presentations, and executive/client-facing technical communication.
