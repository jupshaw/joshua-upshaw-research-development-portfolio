# Research Principles

## Measurement before modeling
A sophisticated model cannot rescue an invalid outcome, poorly defined event, or unavailable feature.

## Separate signal from artifact
Physiological and clinical datasets require explicit quality gates, source authority, and failure analysis.

## Validate under realistic conditions
Group leakage, temporal shift, prevalence, calibration, and operational workload can determine whether a strong retrospective result is useful.

## Treat null findings as information
Unsupported hypotheses narrow the mechanism and improve the next experiment.

## Preserve provenance
Important conclusions should be traceable from source data through transformation, analysis, model/version identity, and reporting.

## Match claims to evidence
Observed, retrospective, simulated, modeled, prospective, and causal claims are not interchangeable.

## Design the next measurement
When a dataset cannot answer the question, the output of the analysis should include the instrumentation and telemetry required to answer it prospectively.
