# Research Governance, Reproducibility, and Traceability

## Traceability model

```text
claim -> requirement -> implementation -> code/configuration -> dataset ->
test -> model/version -> result -> provenance -> limitation -> release decision
```

## Research-governance practices

- IRB and human-subjects procedures
- informed consent and records-based research controls
- de-identification and minimum-necessary processing
- source lineage and provenance
- versioned datasets and models
- explicit acceptance criteria
- reproducible analysis documents
- technical claims review
- separation of retrospective, simulated, proposed, and prospective evidence

## Governance experience

The portfolio combines hands-on study execution with formal research-service experience, including service on a university Institutional Review Board Research Ethics Committee from 2021 to 2025. That perspective reinforces a practical approach to research governance: participant protection and evidentiary quality are design constraints, not final-stage paperwork.

## Peer review and evidence appraisal

Ad hoc journal reviewing adds another layer of methodological quality control. Reviewing requires assessing whether study design, analysis, interpretation, and claims are mutually consistent, then communicating concerns in a form that improves the work.
