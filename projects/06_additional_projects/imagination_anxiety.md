# Imagination and Anxiety During COVID-19

**Status:** Coauthor, peer-reviewed  
**Publication:** *Creativity Research Journal* (2021)  
**DOI:** https://doi.org/10.1080/10400419.2020.1871549

## Design

Two samples were analyzed:

- College sample: **N = 101**
- International community sample: **N = 61**

The work examined imagination frequency/qualities, anxiety before and during the pandemic, and loneliness.

## Results pattern

- More frequent imagination was associated with greater anxiety during the pandemic.
- Loneliness amplified anxiety change among frequent imaginers in the college sample.
- A valence-related interaction appeared in Study 1 but did **not** replicate in Study 2.

## What this adds

The project illustrates two-sample reasoning, interaction analysis, affective individual differences, and the importance of distinguishing replicated from nonreplicated findings.
